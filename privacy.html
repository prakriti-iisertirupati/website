Privacy & consent

How we handle information

Prakriti is a student club, not a company. This page explains what happens to the information people share with us through this website and through the field programme, in plain language.

Last updated: August 2026

The website itself

This site does not use tracking cookies. There is no analytics, no advertising and no third-party script watching visitors. The only thing stored in your browser is a small preference for text size if you use the A− / A / A+ buttons, and that never leaves your device.

The forms on this site

None of the forms on this site store what you type on any server of ours. When you press send, your own email application opens with the message already written, addressed to prakriti@sac.iisertirupati.ac.in. You then send it yourself. That means the club receives your message from your own address, and you keep a copy in your sent folder.

If you do not have an email application configured on your device, the send button will not do anything visible. In that case, copy the email address from the footer of any page and write to us the usual way.

Farmer reports and phone numbers

The Field Notes form asks for a village, a crop, a description of the problem, and optionally a name and phone number. The phone number matters because it is how the club can carry a researcher's answer back to the farmer — without it, the report becomes one-directional.

Names and phone numbers are never published on this website. The public board shows only the mandal, the crop, an anonymous report ID, and the problem itself. Personal details stay in the club's private records and are used only to close the loop with the person who reported the problem. They are not shared with sponsors, not sold, and not passed to anyone outside the small team handling reports.

School students collecting reports

Part of the field programme involves partner-school students interviewing farmers and filing reports. Because minors are collecting information from adults about their livelihoods, the club follows a few rules that are not optional.

Every partner school signs a written understanding with the club, and one teacher at each school is named as the point of contact for anything to do with the programme. Students take a short training session before they file their first report, covering how to ask for consent, how to write down what someone actually said rather than what they think it meant, and when not to photograph. Field visits happen only with the teacher's knowledge, and the farmer is always told what the report will be used for before any information is written down.

Photographs of people

Photographs of identifiable people — farmers, students, staff, visitors — are only published on this website or on the club's social media with the person's explicit permission. If you have ever appeared in a photograph on our site and would like it taken down, write to us and we will remove it.

Photographs of land

Photographs of someone's land or crop damage are treated the same way as photographs of people: shared publicly only when the farmer has agreed to it. Where a photograph is used to help a researcher diagnose a problem, that use is private and does not become a public post.

Data we do not collect

The club does not run its own database, does not track visits, does not build profiles of anyone, and has no login system. There is nothing you need to opt out of, because there is nothing to opt into.

Corrections and takedowns

If something on this site is wrong about you, or something you contributed should not be there any more, write to prakriti@sac.iisertirupati.ac.in and it will be corrected or removed. This includes farmer reports: even after a report is on the board, the farmer or the student who filed it can ask for it to be taken down.

A note on the institute

Prakriti is a student club running under the Student Activity Centre of IISER Tirupati. It is not an advisory service of the institute, and nothing on this site should be treated as an official recommendation from IISER Tirupati. Where a researcher answers a farmer's report, that answer is the researcher's own view and not a formal institutional position.
