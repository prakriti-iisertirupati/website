Field notes

A farmer describes a problem. A student writes it down properly. We send it to a researcher who works on that exact thing. The answer comes back in Telugu, without jargon, delivered by the same student.

Tell us what is going wrong

Fill in what you can. Only the village and the problem are needed. When you send it, your email app opens with everything filled in, ready to go to us.

We never publish or sell your phone number. Only the problem, the mandal and the crop appear on the public board.

What makes a report useful

When it started — “three weeks after sowing” beats “recently”. How much is affected — one corner of the field, or all of it. What you already tried, including what the shop recommended. A photograph of the damage. Borewell depth, if the problem is water.

Students filing on someone’s behalf

Put the farmer’s village, not your school’s. Ask before photographing anyone’s face or land. Write what they said, not what you think they meant. One report per problem — two problems means two reports.

Open problems

Every report on this board is anonymised. Mandal and crop only.

For researchers

Open to anyone, anywhere: agronomy, soil science, hydrology, entomology, plant pathology, agricultural economics, extension.

For schools

Your students become the network

We train a batch of students to interview farmers and file reports properly — consent, photographs, plain-language note-taking. It is real scientific practice with a real recipient, and it costs about two hours a month.

We bring the training session, the report format and the certificates. You bring the students and one teacher who will answer our calls.
